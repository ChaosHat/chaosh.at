When I was [creating the canon for the site](/e/canon-premiere/), I really struggled with narrowing some of these down. My mental model for the canon, aside from just a personal exercise for fun, is something I think of as a gamer business card. I want to be able to give you this short list of games and very quickly understand where I'm coming from, what I like or value, and use that as context for the rest of my writing. 

By discussing my mental rubric here, you'll understand how those things get on the canon. None of these rules are hard and fast. Some of this is just looking at what's on there and seeing what *feels* correct as a collection of ten games. Some things will get on there and break these rules. 

My first example is Baldur's Gate 2. It's a tremendous game and one of my favorites. I have fond memories of taking the giant spiral-bound manual it came with in my backpack to read on the bus to school. I battled with this for a long time. It's so influential to who I am now on so many levels. I'm not sure I would be a TTRPG player without this. So should I put this on the canon instead of its successor, Baldur's Gate 3? Doesn't BG3 owe BG2 everything?

I say thee nay! BG2 is great. I think it STILL holds up. However, Baldur's Gate 3 builds upon that foundation and realizes it even further. It doesn't owe BG2 anything for the legacy. It *inherits* that legacy, does it justice, and then advances the genre.

That leads us to the first rule: games don't get credit for legacy. I don't want games to be unassailable due to nostalgia, or what the genre "owes" them. This is video game canon rent seeking behavior and I refuse to enable it. That's also how you get ten games on a list that never change because of the sheer weight of what they accomplished. The point of the list, from the jump, is to be something that grows and evolves as my tastes and the games change! This should be a fresh list that describes how I am currently as a gamer and also serve as some modern recommendations. 

The next game we're looking at is Mass Effect (the series (no it's not cheating they were released as one game as the Legendary Edition (yes that counts))). Mass Effect is such a towering achievement of ambition and scope: a three-part game where your decisions mattered not only in that game, but in the games to come is so incredible. In the modern era of gaming where investors are impatient and developers are sold, shuttered, and projects can change hands this is an even more impressive accomplishment. 

The next rule is therefore: beatability. In the spirit of keeping this a living, updated list, the question is will anything beat this? The list stops saying anything interesting about me if the ten games don't evolve. I have to be able to envision a realistic world where something can do better. Its incredible scope means that a real competitor to that slot would take at least a decade to finish, if ever! That's why it's a favorite, and not canonical. 

Fallout: New Vegas was on the list for a long time. It's the best Fallout game by a mile and I love that series. I've played every game in the mainline series (and also Tactics). So why is there no Fallout representation? 

It fails the next rule: derivability. Anyone who sees this list with Baldur's Gate 3 and Cyberpunk 2077 is going to be able to figure out that Hat likes CRPGs. A lot. Fantasy, sci-fi, first-person, top-down, whatever. This list needs to represent me as a gamer and span a lot of different stuff. I can't burn that many slots on RPGs just because I like them. They each need to at least say something different and New Vegas doesn't quite reach that. 

This isn't a strict genre quota, but because these games all answer the same questions about me, they don't further the conversation or say anything new. We could imagine Steam recommending New Vegas to someone who owned/played the other games on this list, and that means it's not doing anything new. 

Another great example of how this worked in practice was Pokemon Heart Gold and Soul Silver. Literally as I was writing its entry in the Canon Premiere post, I knew it didn't belong on there because I was struggling to write the paragraph of why. It was earning that spot heavily through nostalgia and obligation to include a mainline Pokemon game. It's something that I don't think anything could beat because of that, and therefore it didn't earn its spot. And "Hat likes Pokemon" is derivable because of all the other RPGs and Pokopia (and also just demographics I mean come on it's one of the biggest media properties in the world). 

When I stripped all of that away, what made Gold and Silver memorable to me was pretty thin: content. The value of the second act after beating the Elite Four was amazing, but not something to keep it on the canon.

The goal is for each slot to say something different, to let each one earn its way onto this list, and in that way show who I am as a gamer, what I like and value. The rubric is here to help me figure out ten different ways to say that. Ten good games is just a top ten list; the canon is an attempt to have a conversation with you and give you the tl;dr about me. So when you're reading my posts you can say "of course Hat likes that, it's just like this thing in BG3."
