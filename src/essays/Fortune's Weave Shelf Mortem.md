I wish there was something that tracked a live graph of how much I liked this game as I played measured in millifuns (yes, we're metric on this blog). It would be a real rollercoaster. There's the initial optimism, the "heck yeah we're Fire Embleming" that starts good. Then we arrive at the slow downward "oh no this might not be for me." Then about ten hours or so in I got to "this game is not good and I don't like it," which was the nadir. Afterwards it climbs steadily and there was a moment where I thought "yes, this is *the best Fire Emblem*, this is my new canon" before I settled into my final thoughts on it.

And yes, my final thoughts. I am pausing this game in the middle of act 3, chapter 2, but I think it's incredibly unlikely that my thoughts will meaningfully change. There's not enough time for it. Sure, it could go up or down a little, but I don't think it could move a whole letter grade. So what are those final thoughts?

This game is only okay.

And that's a weird stance to take for a game I have around 130 hours on. I think about those Steam reviews: "thumbs down, this game is bad, total waste of time. 90 hours played at time of review." But there's some nuance here. Fortune's Weave is so good that it's ultimately its own worst enemy. The highs make the valleys seem so deep, vast, and hard to ignore. It's a victim of its own ambition and hubris, it tells Icarus "hold my beer." But let's start with the highs before we dig into it because there are very high highs.

*The best.*

Leda's story is really unique for Fire Emblem and her cast is one of the best in any game. She's also by far the most interesting unit that has ever existed in Fire Emblem. She has so many options in combat where she can be so impactful in different ways: she's a capable fighter on her own, being able to use swords and having balanced offensive stats and high speed that make her a threat with a regular or Levin sword. She also has bow access, letting her take out fliers. She can dance to let another unit go again, or she can sing and provide powerful buffs to the whole squad. She's wonderful.

The map design for main story maps is very good and creative, especially towards the end of act 1, the paralogues, and act 2. A lot of these were interesting puzzles and act 2 became challenging enough to really make me try and think about it. Sand is also just a runaway improvement over Divine Pulse. It gives you a lot of other options for how to use the ability and can let you feel smart and proactive instead of just being a redo button.

> [!aside] New to Fortune's Weave?
> Fortune's Weave follows the path of four main characters, Flame Lords, who compete in the Heroic Games, a tournament where the winner is granted a wish by the Divine Emperor. Our four lords are: 
> 
> - Cai, an idealistic young boy fighting for his family's future
> - Theodora, a queen focused on the prosperity and future of her people
> - Leda, a mysterious woman with a revenge list scrawled in blood
> - Dietrich, washed up from a foreign country only living for the thrill of the fight.
> 
> There's also Eshmel, a mysterious figure created by the player who travels through time to assist the Flame Lords, becoming the main character in the final act. 
> 
> It has a three acts, the first takes us through each lord's path, the second is a shared story where each lord has some unique moments, and a final act where the lords unite to fight the boss.
> 
> The story is told in chapters, each one anchored by a main story battle, with variable amounts of free time between the primary missions. Free time is used to increase the bonds of your characters, train, do additional skirmishes, or delve into dungeons with jrpg style battles. 
> 
> Divine Sand is a meta power that can be spent to rewind time in combat or gain powerful temporary buffs to hit harder or be more defensive. 

The combat mechanics are also a step in the right direction if not yet perfect. I am a weapon triangle lover, but if they're going to stop using the triangle then at least they're carving out niches for each weapon. They also solved the issue in prior games where the best option was to jam everyone into flying classes, more advancement paths feel viable. Durability loss on combat arts only is a good happy medium to make durability matter but not something to stress about. 

The actual story is good, if convoluted a bit and weighed down with so many proper nouns it's hard to keep everything straight, but still I craved MORE. I would have loved a little in game dictionary that could pop up during cutscenes so I could remember who the heck this character is and where else in the story I saw them. I wanted more supports, more interactions with these characters.

But as for why this is short of the canon? I just have too many issues with it and so many of them seem self inflicted. They're issues that feel like they were either unfinished for money or deadline reasons, or things that I don't understand how they thought literally *anyone* would like this. A good litmus test for Fire Emblem mechanics is how does this help me with the core of what the franchise is about: the story of allies forging bonds in the fires of war told through support and tactical battles.

The game is oddly weighted and inconsistent as a whole, it's more several games roughly bolted on to each other. Act 1 is so disjointed and there's so much of it that it makes the game bottom heavy. If they wanted to do this, they should have made the lords and their stories based around parallel play, making it easier to see how everything is interwoven, but that has issues with burying the player in mechanics and making it take 100 hours or more to get to the second act. At least the way it is now a player can critical path one lord and move on.

Dungeons are so thin. The combat is uninteresting and they know that. That's why they put in a skip battle option but there's still so much of them. They feel like someone just saw the critical acclaim of Persona 5 and then decided they needed to copy it and just latched onto Mementos being the thing Fire Emblem doesn't have. But Mementos sucks, it's the worst part of Persona 5! It's just drudgery, but at least Persona's combat is interesting.

The overworld map mechanics are...plentiful. Too much so, and this comes to a head in the third act when it says "hey here's all of them at once and then some more for good measure, try not to have your head explode." They also aren't all equal in time and attention. Leda's mechanic feels tacked on, like they had to do SOMETHING for her because everyone else had one (but this was actually one I liked because I was so burnt out on elaborate overworld mechanics). Dietrich's mechanic invites you to do more generic skirmishes which is not this game's strong suit (1v1 battles on the tactical maps too? Who thought that was interesting?)

Theodora and Cai's unique mechanics are the ones I'd be most interested to see fully fleshed out in a main game where they can be the star of the show, but in this game I mostly forgot about Theodora's battalions (or relegated them to farming duty). Cai's mechanic feels like the mounted units are balanced against that making them worse in the three other campaigns.

That's ultimately the story of Fortune's Weave's new mechanics. When I play Fortune's Weave, I feel like I'm demoing a thousand different things that could be fleshed out into the focus of a whole game and I'm overloaded with systems that aren't quite finished.

It's the same with the stories. So many of these stories are rushed, there isn't proper resolution or time for them to breathe. The stories are forced to fit into equal boxes because they have to be paced and given an equal amount of content to the other three flame lords but they don't all need the same things. I would kill (ironically) for a Leda solo game that would give the proper time to depict her descent and redemption and have more space to explore the ramifications on her friends. 

Another story issue that grinds my gears: there are plot critical cutscenes that are totally missable! Baozi is a character introduced in the main thrust of Dietrich's story and then his resolution is done entirely by missable cutscenes! From my perspective, until I saw it on youtube, it was a real "Poochy died on the way to his home planet" except I didn't even get that resolution! He's just never in any more scenes after a certain point.

The game later feels like Theodora is meant to be the main character, act 2 is the Theodora show. Everyone else is relegated to being a side character, commenting in little scenes that you can tell are meant to be swapped out for other characters without actually interfering with the narrative. They're like ghosts who can see but are unable to interact with the physical world. It makes me wonder why we even bothered pretending the other characters are on the same level as the actual ruler of a nation, surely a game that tried to make these characters equal participants could have made them more vital to the story.

Act 2 is closer to how I would like Fire Emblem to be, it reduces the time between the meat of these games, the story missions, but it's too threadbare on the other end. I'm not some kind of GBA era purist who thinks these should just be battles with brief talking head exposition scenes in between. The modern games add real benefits to the formula. Things like meals and gifting, while they could be streamlined, help with an issue in the GBA games: gaining support was annoying! You only earned it when the units physically stood next to each other on the battlefield. A way to have the characters bond out of combat helped further the narrative that these characters were becoming closer, cared about each other, and then also fix the difficulty of unlocking those supports. Win win!

Compare this to say, weapon skill, where now you can train this in the arena (or school in Three Houses) but this was something you automatically gained from just playing the game, you improved skill by battling. This means that games have to be balanced around the fact that you can grind skill outside of battles so they have to reduce the in combat rate. They took something I got for free and made some hoops for me to jump through. There are some benefits to being able to train on the side, in helping characters supplement a weak skill or allowing them to work towards an unconventional class, but that's not really how the game presents the usage of it. It's just keeping up with the Joneses and I wasn't disappointed that it was gone in the second act, it fails the litmus test from earlier. 

Then in act 3 Eshmel comes in and takes the reins from the lords, which feels...really unearned. Eshmel pushes everyone to the side (now Theodora knows how it feels) and is this flat, uninteresting character when we just spent time bonding with everyone else. I cared more and was more attached to the other four Flame Lords who don't even get their own paths. I'd much rather get more time with Talimun or Anatolia than have Eshmel. 

![[Pasted image 20261014112735.png]]

*Leda's face is exactly how I feel about Perfect Bird Time.*

Perfect Bird Time is where this should have been fixed but it does nothing to help us understand why the characters would feel so much affinity toward Eshmel. The Eshmel A supports have such a creepy familiarity with Eshmel, as though she's Leda's old friend and confidant catching up after a few years. Leda, Eshmel was a bird you fed a couple dozen times and were weirdly emotionally reliant on. You got wildly upset when the bird would sing instead of nodding. She's not your best friend and it feels fake. 

I'm also just over the silent player insert protagonist but if you're going to do it, do it well. This should have been a game where Eshmel was the primary character that you had on every route, going back in time over and over to keep fixing the past until you get it right. Instead, Perfect Bird Time is a bandaid fix that has memeable upside for the marketing team. It just feels added in because we obviously need to have tea time for those fans.

And that is truly the original sin of Fortune's Weave. It needs to throw in every idea, nothing too bold, too big or too small, and no critical examination of does this need to be here? What is it accomplishing? Is it doing that well? Who cares. Throw in mounts and battalions and bird time and dungeons, and and and. The more the merrier. 

I like this game a lot! I couldn't have played this long if it was bad. It's just I can see where this could have been truly amazing and instead it just feels simultaneously too big and not enough. Well made and underdone. It's like an amazing quality steak that was cut much too thick and cooked way too hot so it's burnt on the outside cold on the inside but there's a middle band where it's just perfect. When you bite into that part you just see what the whole thing could have been.

I hope that by letting this steak rest, it'll even out and I'll have more patience for the game's foibles and issues. I'm also excited for whatever comes next. Hopefully the lesson Intelligent Systems learns is what works and what doesn't and it can take the best from here and refine it. My fear is they'll just keep making it bigger and bigger.
