I've recently been noticing something in a lot of the fan communities I'm a part of. I went to look something up for clarification in Sovereign Tower and noticed there is just an absolute *deluge* of AI generated guide sites for it. Similarly, in the retro handheld/emulation space, there are so many people developing apps for dual screen handhelds to take advantage of the second screen, or de/recomping things to run natively on Android. There's so much of this and it's coming so fast.

And it's exhausting. I feel like I'm being technologically gish galloped. 

My opinion on AI is mixed. I would describe myself as a skeptic overall, but I can see the value of it in some places as a tool. On the other hand, I think it's too much, too soon and it's being put everywhere, regardless of whether it needs to be or any consideration of the downsides. It's a powerful tool, but like powerful tools it requires judgment and thoughtfulness on when, where, and why to apply it. In these cases, I think it has some positives but a lot more negatives. 

I want to believe the best intentions of the people here. They're excited, they have a need, they fixed it, and now they're excited to share it with the world. There wasn't a guide or an app for this use case, they made it, and they bet other people want it too so they decided to provide it for others. Now with AI they finally feel empowered to go out there and do it because the AI can compensate for whatever gap they had that prevented them from doing it in the first place. And it's so fast and easy! 

I think that can be awesome. I love that passion and enthusiasm. But that lack of friction is coming at a cost. It feels like AI lowering the friction is enabling some ADHD behavior (and I say that as a proud ADHD haver). The ability to build and iterate rapidly without friction lets you get that hit of dopamine very quickly without having to consider what you're doing and why and that has negative effects for both the builder and the intended end user.

We all know AI is prone to hallucination. When you're shipping a website that has the off the shelf AI web designer visual language and all the hallmarks of LLM generated text, it doesn't inspire confidence that anyone checked this over. There's no sign of authorial intent or rigor, a vision to back it up. 

But more than that, this is a community of enthusiast tinkerers that has historically loved to post screenshots of a triple A game running at 30 fps with frame gen on saying "I can't believe this is playable on Steam Deck!" That's not playable and I know you don't actually think that because we never see posts from anyone saying they completed those triple A games. The screenshots are always from menus or the first ten minutes of the game. 

It's impossible for me to know if you have a reasonable definition of playable or you vetted this Android native decomp beyond the first ten minutes. I'm supposed to just sink my time into this 40 hour game because you said it's good just to find out my save got corrupted 20 minutes in? You built this so quickly it's hard for me to believe it's well tested. When it takes more time it makes me believe you stuck with this so long to make something that works instead of just shipping whatever you did in an afternoon of vibe coding with Dangerously Skip Permissions on.

These projects can ship updates really frequently. Were these stability updates? New features? Will it invalidate anything I've already set up, or will I have to learn something new? Because of this rapid change and turnover, it's hard for me to know when I should care or pay attention. I'd rather just jump in when it's good and you have a version you're proud of. I want to play these games and be certain it'll be a good experience and I'm just not confident it'll work end to end compared to something like RetroArch which isn't perfect, but is something I have faith in. 

The churn has another side to it, the fear of abandonment. What happens when this is 80% feature complete and this comes down to bug hunting and less glamorous things? Are you going to keep jamming in features to chase that dopamine high, or will you just abandon the project when the novelty wears off for the next new shiny thing? This is another instance where the effort shows the intent. If someone tested it and continues working on it past an afternoon or a weekend of vibe coding, that proves commitment.

Since creating is so fast and easy, people don't bother to look for prior art before striking off on their own. Hey, maybe I don't need to start from the ground up, maybe I could improve an existing project with a PR or if they don't want to, I could just fork it! Instead we end up with a handful of Sovereign Tower sites that all do more or less the same thing, or a giant list of AYN Thor second screen dashboard utilities. 

All of this builds to where everyone undermines what everyone is doing. I'm not going to sift through all the Sovereign Tower sites to figure out which is the good one with accurate data. They'll just all not get my usage. I'm not going to test all your different apps and compare and contrast their features and issues. By everyone rushing to fill the need they all just drown each other out. What's worse is they probably eat up all the SEO oxygen from the people who aren't rushing to just churn something out and then as the sites get abandoned their carcasses just bloat out the search results. 

These people could have pooled their efforts and found each other, another person who's passionate and shares their interests. They could make something better, because I believe none of us is as good as all of us. And then they could bring in more people who maybe couldn't contribute, but would use and be a part of something. A community.

It reminds me of that scene in Home Alone where Kevin tricks the Wet Bandits into thinking there's a raucous holiday party happening in his house with all the cardboard cutouts. It looks like something cool and fun is happening but it's really just one person pulling the strings of their agents, wishing their friends and family were with them.
