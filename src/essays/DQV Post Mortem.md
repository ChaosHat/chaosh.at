What's the best way to get into an old series? 

As I've been doing a lot of retro gaming backlog stuff this year, this question has become more and more front of mind to me. How do we choose what to play, and how should we recommend things to newcomers? Recently, I played both Dragon Quest XI S and Dragon Quest V and I think this really encapsulates the dilemma here. 

If you search around, DQXIS is pretty reliably called the best jumping on point to the franchise and I can see why. Its presentation is lovely, it's well voice acted, it has the most up to date mechanics and quality of life benefits. It's even recommended ahead of newer remakes like the HD-2D remakes and VII Reimagined. What I read online was that XIS is the one to start with, and if you like that you'll like the others.

So that's what led me to Dragon Quest V, and so is it true? I liked XIS, do I like V? Dragon Quest V is a perfectly fine game that ultimately let me down. Some of this is the delta between its ambitions/how it was described to me and ultimately what it was. DQV suffers from how big its goals are and how hard it is to fully execute on that in the time allowed with the technology it has. That's worse because I have the counterfactual of what it could be right there in XIS. 

In many ways, its ambition is so much higher than DQXIS which is, narratively, extremely bog standard. DQV wants to tell this story about the growth of a character and their tragic and epic life in three acts and having a family and reclaiming a kingdom and avenging your parents. It wants to tell so much and so much of that is pretty good!

But it just lets that ambition down. The wife choice is such a huge thing at the time and feels really important! I wanted to really consider both from a story and gameplay perspective, who do I need to include? But that choice doesn't end up mattering so much because your wife is taken from you so quickly and not returned until the end. 

It also does this thing where it wants to primarily build out the characters through optional and missable party chat which I think is a gigantic mistake. There was so much charm in talking to your party members and it made the kids more endearing, there's just not much of it and because it's just party chat, there's no back and forth, it's just kind of idle and forgettable commentary. Not only that, but similar to the wife issue, there are just long stretches of the game where the characters you care about are missing. You just have the monsters who have nothing to say at all. 

And lastly, I'm not sure how playable I would have found this game without a ton of modern conveniences. I consistently felt like this game dragged on a LOT and it felt like being able to 2x fast forward through some of the cutscenes was necessary. This just felt like the speed it should be running at, it does things so slowly. Being able to save whenever and increase my xp rate to reduce grinding were huge contributing factors to being able to finish this.

Part of why those modern conveniences feel necessary is that this game just does not have much going on mechanically. DQXIS felt thin, but this is beyond that, there's not much there. Itemization is a linear path, there are no build options. There's a little bit to do with party composition of the monsters you take, but this is a bit of a false choice. Most of them are outclassed by the named characters like your kids and wife, and of the monsters, some of them are just head and shoulders better than others. I got slime knight early and never really had to move off him.

I still plan on playing Dragon Quest games, which I guess says something about the quality of the game/series but I think my expectations are much more tempered. 

So, how do we know what to choose as a starting point? Do you start with the best possible entry knowing that you may only get exactly one shot at a recommendation, but leaving them let down when they hit the back catalog? Do you show the newbie maybe the second or third best, hoping it's good enough to get them intrigued and give them room to see how much better it gets? 

I still don't know. But, if I were to recommend a game in this series to someone, I couldn't pick DQV because just mechanically it's so slow and it doesn't show you anything new beyond the first handful of hours. Right now, of my two choices, it has to be DQXIS by a landslide.
