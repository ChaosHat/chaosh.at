A real theme I've been noting since I started reflecting on games more regularly is just the question of ambition and scope. How do you decide what to change, what are your sacred cows of the series and what can you play with? What is too much at once? This is overdue and as such my memory has degraded a little bit, but I wanted to consolidate some thoughts about the DS Castlevanias I played earlier this year through this lens.

I found Dawn of Sorrow pretty forgettable. It runs back a winning concept in Soma and the souls system but ultimately doesn't do much to add to that. The seals thing is so derided that there's a fan patch to remove it. The plot definitely cannot hold up to Aria of Sorrow, and that's saying something: these plots are pretty thin to start. Soma was a new and interesting character in Aria, the revelation that he was the new heir to Dracula's power was a twist. Aria also dropped some real lore on how all of this works, with Dracula's relationship to Chaos and why he keeps coming back.

*Dawn of Sorrow was not a glow up for Soma.*

Order of Ecclesia is the opposite. Where Dawn of Sorrow plays it pretty conservatively, Order takes some risks. It deviates from the established post Symphony of the Night structure by bringing back the old linear levels of the original games and also radically changes the way the equipment and power gaining work via the glyphs system. 

The glyphs are a great idea and an interesting evolution on Soma's souls mechanic. You commonly gain them from enemies and you can absorb their power and make them your own. The glyphs each have typing, (slashing, bludgeoning, fire, lightning, etc) and certain enemies are weak to certain flavors. This encourages you to think about each fight and what you need to give yourself an advantage. I felt encouraged to switch to bludgeoning weapons even just to take down skeleton trash mobs. 

The glyphs are unfortunately let down by the controls. You can assign your primary attack glyphs to X and Y and you can even double up on the same glyph to double your dps by spamming the buttons alternatingly. In theory, this is an interesting trade off. Do you use the same weapons to make it easier to increase your damage since both weapons operate the same way, or do you try to get some versatility and halve your output? 

This doesn't work out that way though. I nearly always doubled up because the dps loss felt big, but also it was just mentally easier to do that. I would have preferred it if they had balanced the game around not being able to double up glyphs and just having you equip two different options. This would have also removed the slightly awkward playstyle of having to rapidly alternate the buttons.  

Another control issue is that you can swap between three profiles pretty quickly but I kind of always forgot exactly what was on which. Three profiles wasn't enough to just set and forget them, I had to keep changing them up. I can imagine a world where this game has a radial menu on an analog stick where you can assign a helpful icon to each of six positions. You absolutely want to use the profiles since returning to the menu every time to swap out a piercing weapon for this given monster would be a pain, but as is it feels like half a quality of life improvement.

*Shanoa is just cool.*

The linear levels however did not pay off for me, I found them to be kind of a slog. But nothing ventured nothing gained and overall I can respect OoE for taking chances. And Shanoa is a cool character, her design is great and the concept of a different Dracula management group is a nice shakeup from the familial Belmonts and Alucards who normally handle this. 

The DS Castlevanias are both games that are fine. I wouldn't tell anyone to go out of their way to play them but if you're a fan of the genre they're worth trying out. But what do they tell us about what you can change? 

These are my favorite questions, the ones without hard answers. But these games both feel like Castlevania games, so we know they didn't miss any sacred cows. They have spooky gothic settings where you kill Dracula and the mechanics feature backtracking and power gaining. Messing with the shape of some of the equipment mechanics doesn't change that soul, even if they have mixed results. Order comes closest to messing with the cows with the linear levels, but they're mostly early and get out of the way, it's not enough to ruin what I like about the series. They're mostly a bit of spice here or there that I don't like, but they don't spoil the dish. 

You might be wondering where Belmont's Curse fits into all of this, and well, it doesn't fit into this essay but it has a lot to do with this conversation.
