Some of the games on my canon are quite old so I wanted to circle back and elaborate on why they're here and why others aren't and again, continue to peel back the decision space on how I consider the canon. I wanted to wait to write about Awakening until I had a good crack at Fortune's Weave and could really decide whether or not this article would have been "Why Fortune's Weave is on the Canon." It's not though. It won't be. I mean, maybe that DLC will be REAL good and they'll fix all my issues and I'll revisit. 

![[Pasted image 20261022215838.png]]

*The Shepherds*

I'm getting distracted. Let's start, but let's not start with "why Awakening?", but why "any Fire Emblem?"

I love a good tactical game, video or otherwise. Between your crafts, (War or Star), or your X-'s (Com or Wing Miniatures Game), this is something I've always sought out. There's something so satisfying about maneuvering your troops, fighting, balancing strategy with the immediate needs, and doing the problem solving to understand how these things can achieve that objective. There's also just an inherent tactility and ownership, this is MY unit, I move MY troops. I feel responsible for them. 

The best of these games feel like plate spinning, you're trying to manage this thing you need to do offensively while protecting yourself and not overextending and you never have enough to do everything you need. You have to stretch, you have to take calculated risks. 

Fire Emblem has always felt like the best version of this. You not only have the tactics, but you gain a campaign but more than that, a story. And stories are where Fire Emblem, starting from Blazing Blade (the first one I played) has always succeeded.

![[Pasted image 20261022221019.png]]

*The titular Awakening.*

No, not the actual A plot stories which have various levels of competence. It's the character stories. That's where Fire Emblem differentiated itself from something like an X-COM or a Final Fantasy Tactics for me. These aren't generic protagonists recruited at random, they're authored characters with personality and an arc. 

That arc is further compounded by the support system, a brilliant way to show how the fires of war can forge a bond between characters. It's not about whatever evildoer wants to take over the world, it's about how Eliwood, Lyn, and Hector become friends. How the relationship of Chrom and Robin grows. 

Fire Emblem Awakening is the best because it is, at its core, focused on what Fire Emblem is and should be: it's about using the tactics to represent warfare and how those characters bond. Awakening takes that and builds it into the story and the mechanics. 

First, the pair up mechanic does this directly. It lets your characters fight side by side (or in the same space) to attack and defend together. We can see directly in the battle how these characters help each other, a last second defense to protect their ally when things seemed grim, or joining in for a combined attack to defeat a foe. These are the moments when those bonds are built, and this helps the characters build that support level. 

This leads directly to the kid mechanic. When your characters gain an S support with someone, they get married and have a kid who, due to the time travel conveniences of the plot, is instantly grown up and ready to fight! Your choice of who supports and fights with who directly leads to who falls in love and creates the next generation.

![[Pasted image 20261022220840.png]]

*This is what peak gameplay looks like.*

And this choice is not only cosmetic (although it does change the kid's appearance some) but it changes what support conversations they have, different conversations with their variable parentage. Kids also inherit stats and abilities from their parents so again, the story reinforces the mechanics which reinforces the story. 

The class system in this era is also the exact right amount of flexibility. You can't reclass into anything you want so you can't just lead everyone down the same broken build, but it still gives you more freedom than Blazing Blade which is one seal, two choices, that's it. 

I mostly intended this to be a celebration of Awakening, but I think it's worth talking about the other options I considered. 

Blazing Blade was another hot contender for the canon, but the older games struggle more to enable those support conversations, it's just harder to build them up. The only way you can do it is just by fighting, there's no additional way to build them up via gifts or meals. Additionally, while they do earn support by fighting side by side, it's a bit more literal than Awakening's Pair Up mechanic. They just stand there, we don't actually see them supporting each other in combat. I do love it though for the gorgeous sprite art and the simplicity. It just gets you in and out of battles so quickly. There aren't chores to it. 

![[Pasted image 20261022220942.png]]

*It pays to pair up.*

Three Houses was the other primary candidate, its story and characters are among the best. Its problem is mostly mechanical. It feels poorly balanced, even hard isn't that hard and the freedom to make anyone any class flattens the character building. You just make everyone a flier. This is exacerbated by the removal of the weapon triangle, since there's not a lot of tradeoff to everyone focusing on one weapon type from the same classes. And while the chores do address some issues with the more classic style of Fire Emblem (meals help build support, weapon training can make it easier to get characters into harder builds) they mostly just feel like...well chores. 

Why not Fates? Fates (especially Conquest) carries over (and fixes!) some of the issues with Pair Up and keeps the kid mechanic (although it is much sillier) so why wasn't it a higher contender? The plot is so bad. Just...brain bleeding out your ears bad. If in some world we married Fates' mechanics with Three Houses' story, we'd be cooking. 

I'm not going to write about Fortune's Weave, I've done enough of that already, which you can see here. 

So, to end this on a high note and get back to that celebration, here are some more things I like about Awakening.

![[Pasted image 20261022221059.png]]

*That's my Marth.*

- Lucina is a badass. One of my favorite characters from the series. 
- Robin is my favorite blank self insert character, partially because they feel a little less blank. You can have a kid and have support conversations with them!
- Everything about Owain, that lovable dork, and the fact that he comes back in Fates. 
- I like when the beasty characters are included, the Manaketes and Taguels are always interesting. 
- This is the right amount of world map. You can do some grinding, some buying, but it's not onerous. Extremely optional. 
- Who doesn't love a fun time travel story with heroes from the future returning to save the past?
- I love how they still use pixel art sprites on the overworld. Fire Emblem has never looked better than when it had that gorgeous sprite art on the GBA and at least they give us this much. 

![[Pasted image 20261022220633.png]]

*Owain is too cool.*

The only knock I have against Awakening is that it is a terrible recommendation for someone getting into the game being a dual screen game. It would be such a phenomenal entry point for people. It desperately needs a remaster for single screen devices. This game is at least a quarter of the reason I bought an Ayn Thor.
