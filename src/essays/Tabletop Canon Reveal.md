Since expanding the site for more mediums, those mediums will all each get their own canons! My rubric is essentially similar to the one for video games and here's a post that celebrates the initial roster of the canon. 
## Concordia
Concordia is a monument to simplistic but deep design and it has so much freshness just from new maps. It's a game that, to me, represents what this hobby is at the sweetest spot. Easy to learn, hard to master, lots of depth. I love the card cycling and the scoring methods and the tension of when to pull your hand back. 
## Fromage
For a while this was Tzolk'in, which is still an all timer, but I think Fromage gets you almost all of the same temporal thinkiness but with the killer bonus of being more streamlined and allowing for simultaneous turns. It plays faster which means it's so much easier to get a good game in. I also appreciate a non-traditional theme (although Tzolk'in is still unique) and that it's a little less intimidating for new players. 
## Rokoko
Speaking of non-traditional themes, Rokoko is another banger. A fancy dress making game is so fun, and this has what feels like an early version of the worker placement/deckbuilding in Lost Ruins of Arnak and Dune: Imperium. It can be surprisingly vicious as you fight for locations in the palace and different materials and patterns. 
## Android: Netrunner
The greatest competitive constructed deckuilding card game of all time, bar none. I love the asymmetrical play which gives you two different puzzles to consider. The tension in the runs is so delightful from both sides. As a corp you're desperately trying to keep your cool and not reveal anything to the runner, and as the runner you're sweating, hoping you have what it takes to get in and it's not a trap. I also love how the IDs offer such a varied deck building hook within the factions. 
## Forest Shuffle
My current favorite tableau card game. A nice, naturalist theme and fun combos. It plays quickly and I always love multi use cards and the tension that comes when in order to play the cards you have to spend the others in your hand. It forces tough choices but also interacts with how you acquire cards. Your opponents can draw from the discard which can really limit the cards you're willing to discard. No, I don't need this, but I'll be giving it to my opponent. With good timing, you can wait until the clearing is full and the discard is...super discarded, but it can be painful to wait. 
## Hot Streak
This is, right now, the best party game. It sets up quick and has a bright, fun presentation. It's so easy for everyone to enjoy and is always a delight. It gives players the right amount of information to feel like you've got the inside scoop but you can always be surprised by the deck not panning out.
## Heat: Pedal to the Metal
A gateway game that fits the theme and also scales to be something more interesting if you're looking for depth. For new players it's easy to understand and feel like you're in a real race, and for the more experienced it offers the additional modes and the championship series for extra challenge and customization of your car. The car automa is also well tuned and simple enough to run so even at low player counts you benefit from additional cars to block and draft off of. 
## Arkham Horror LCG
Arkham LCG is a little TTRPG in constructed deck building form. You get that feeling of dungeon crawling, building and optimizing your character over a campaign. The xp mechanic is brilliant and forces you to choose between your greed and safety. Pulling the chaos tokens out of the bag instead of dice as your randomizer is another tension builder and also allows for fun ways for characters to interact with the variance. It's a fun co-op game that lets you agonize and debate the best moves with your partner but is also excellent as a solo experience. 
## River Valley Glassworks
My choice for lightweight abstract gateway game right now. It's fun and cute, plays quick and is easy to learn. The scoring is what makes this good, having to go wide AND tall ensures balance in your approach and gives those high highs and occasional low lows. 
## Arboretum
I *love* a small box card game that plays amazing at two players and is kind of mean. Your Lost Cities, Battle Lines, etc. Arboretum wins because it looks so good and I love how the spatial element of laying your trees out can make you feel so clever. A huge bonus is that you can play up to four with it, and it's still great that way, and when you play with fewer players, you can pick what trees you want in the deck. Does that matter mechanically? No, but the trees look different and you can have a fun chat about the color palette you want for your next game with your partner. 
## Daggerheart 
Daggerheart is the post-OGL "D&D killer" that I've spent the most time on and it's a really good system. I'm pretty confident I would play it over D&D 90% of the time if given the choice because it's simpler, easier to learn, and is a bit more flexible. I have problems with it, no system is perfect, but it moves in the right direction for how I'd like to play which is decidedly less crunchy. The standout part of Daggerheart is the dice system. I'm not sure I've seen a dice system that allows for a wide range of outcomes (but not too wide, looking at you FFG Star Wars) and is so simple. 

So there's the canon. I do expect a bit less turnover on these since board gaming is a hobby that requires more people so it's harder to get a lot of plays on games since I can't just crank them out solo. Having said that, there's a good blend of old and new here and I'm always looking forward to more games.
