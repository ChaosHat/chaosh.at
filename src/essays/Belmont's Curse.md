As I write this, currently, I don't know if Belmont's Curse is a canon game for me over Metroid Dread. I've struggled, I've gone back and forth, and there's a clear cleavage of why this is such a challenging choice for me. And why do these two clash instead of just having both? Having both on doesn't say anything new about me, they're redundant on the business card. They just say "Hat likes Metroidvanias." So what's the case for either? 

![[4231820_20261020224452_1.png]]

*Is Rose the chosen one?*

Metroid Dread is deeper and well, artsier. It has more to say, it's doing different things narratively. This is partially crazy because Dread is so thin on plot. Samus is so silent, most of the plot is driven one sidedly by Samus's AI companion. Belmont's Curse has all kinds of characters for Rose to talk to and bounce off of thanks to the tarot cards, and she just banters more with the villains. 

But Dread does a better job of storytelling through atmosphere. I went on about this in some depth on [that one's article](/e/metroid-dread/). It makes you feel things that Belmont's Curse never did. I would say the experience is, emotionally, pretty flat. 

At the same time, Castlevania feels like it's more opinionated and unique as a setting. Belmont's Curse just looks good and has such a unique style compared to Dread's sterility. Due to that lack of dialogue and exposition, Dread feels generically sci-fi. While maybe it benefits from Dracula being a cultural shorthand to understanding it, Castlevania has a lore to it, it feels more specific. 

![[4231820_20261016223759_1.png]]

*It just looks good.*

But on the other hand, I think Belmont's Curse might just be more fun? It feels tuned for me specifically. 

Curse has better platforming, the segments of it are generally more interesting and more attainable than Dread's. Every moment in there I felt strongly that I could do this, it was just one, two more attempts away from me locking in and getting it done. Even though sometimes it ended up being many more it always felt like I got this.

Whereas Dread had some challenges where I looked at it and said...no. Absolutely not. I'm not grinding this out for a half an hour to get some arcane speed boost storing window exactly right. 

Further, combat is more varied and fun. So much combat in Dread is very samey, the fight locations are very similar and Samus isn't the most mobile character in a lot of ways. While Samus is quick and jumpy and her platforming feels good, Rose's movement is more dynamic, she gets to whip around the map, pull herself to enemies, and airdash from a very early point. She gets a variety of weapons to change up her playstyle, and the spells from her tarot cards. These are ways you can express yourself in the gameplay by having your own preferences. 

![[4231820_20261017142559_1.png]]

*I really appreciated how the library had a low fi beats soundtrack.*

The little challenges in Belmont's Curse are better too. I vastly prefer the Works of Mercy to the insane platforming challenges of Dread. The Works of Mercy felt much more attainable, but also more rewarding. An extra missile pack or super bomb is nice, but it's not needed. And, neither are the Works of Mercy benefits but they go back to that customization, you can get rewarded for using the things you like and they can modify your playstyle. 

Dread's minimal storytelling actually does a better job of signposting, I rarely felt confused about where to go next. Belmont's Curse had long stretches of "well I have no idea what even to do." I know for many diehard fans this is the point, but I personally don't love the feeling of extended flailing on the critical path. Optional stuff? Go nuts. Dread is probably closer to the other extreme of too much handholding, but I would prefer that to the feeling of flailing about and eventually having to look it up online. 

![[4231820_20261016223308_1.png]]

*Me, backtracking aimlessly again.*

Ultimately, I have no idea right now. My gut might nod towards Belmont's Curse but that's because it's new, and the version of Bloody Tears absolutely rocks. This has really caused me to review my goals and the mindset of the canon and its rubric. What is the right amount of turnover? At first I was biased towards more change: challenger wins ties. I thought this was okay because the canon now has a changelog, so other games just graduate into the Hat Hall of Fame. I also don't want the canon to calcify, that's one of the primary concerns here. It's meant to be current. 

But my concern became well, what if the canon and changelog are just a large subset of "the games I played," would that be good? And what if I have this discussion again in a few months when Metroid Ravenous comes out? That feels like too much. I thought about an annual cadence to review the entire canon and all the possible contenders but that felt too static and like I'd forget. It's too slow. 

![[4231820_20261017141016_1.png]]

*I just thought this moment was funny.*

So where do we end here? I think I've decided that essentially, the Court of the Canon will not open until there are enough cases on the docket to consider. When enough builds up and I feel like examining it, then I'll do that. I thought about adding a contender flag to games to help me remember, but then I figured if I don't remember, there's my answer, isn't it? 

And of course, it's my list, my rules, and I can change or break them whenever. So if something really knocks my socks off and I want to promote it immediately. I can do that. As a certain pirate once said, they're more like guidelines.

So Belmont's Curse is a contender and it'll get its day in court. Probably. But as of now, it's a fantastic game, well loved, and an easy recommendation from me.
