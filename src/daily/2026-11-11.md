## The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time
![[Screenshot_20261111-204429.webp]]

*Beelined to this like a sword seeking missile.*

Hat, did you not just start Twilight Princess? Yes. But you know what? I realized Ocarina is in the zeitgeist and I have a friend who's willing to loan the remake to me when they're done, I figured I should replay this beforehand to compare. So I set up Ship of Harkinian on the Thor and got to work. 

Upon starting it, I think this is the single player game I have played and completed the most. Navigating the game just came back like muscle memory. Just grabbed the Kokiri sword and shield, ran through the Deku tree just bam bam bam. Got to Hyrule Castle before I put it down.

![[Screenshot_20261111-210834.webp]]

*I actually didn't realize you could kill Goma so quick.* 

You might wonder why I haven't already gotten the remake. I just don't know how I feel about the concept of it. For me at least. I understand the value in making it available to a new generation, but with Ship of Harkinian and fan graphics mods it looks pretty great and the gameplay still holds up. Ocarina remake seems like a wait for a sale game for me and first party Nintendo games pretty rarely go on sale. Especially Zelda. 
## The Legend of Korra
We've been watching this with my 7 year old daughter, one episode before bedtime and she's really liked it. This privilege has been a real motivator for her. We got to the end of season 1 before I realized oh, I should write about this too. 

I watched Korra once the whole way through a while ago and really liked it. I like how Korra is so different from Aang, she's very refreshing in that way and I think a character I'd be more closely aligned with anyways. She's more brash, prone to action, and in some ways more confident than Aang and in other ways she's still just a kid finding her way in the world.

I forgot they resolve Korra's loss of bending so quickly. It's noticeable because Tenzin tells Mako it'll take her some time to come to terms with the loss and then it's instantly undone. It feels like the kind of thing in other shows it would continue to be an issue into the second season, resolving halfway through or maybe towards the end. 

I'm not sure why they do that. One thought was maybe that's too scary and dark for a kids show, but on the other hand Tarrlok kills himself and Amon on the boat so that can't be it. Still pretty surprised they did that. I think I recall that this show was renewed season to season, so maybe they didn't know they'd have a chance to wrap it up and they wanted to tie it up nicely. 
## Final Fantasy VII Rebirth
So I have to admit, sometimes the little open world chores are decent. I had a little bit of time at the end of the night, got into Rebirth, knocked off a couple world intel things and made some progress. I still don't *love* it for sure. 

I started down the protorelic path and chasing down Beck's Badasses for their return to the remake trilogy. I feel like we're going to see them in part three. I want to give the devs credit for trying to have different things to do, like the stealth section, but to me it just wonders if they tested if it's fun, or if we have different definitions of fun?

So that stealth section it kind of handles poorly. Cloud is slow and plodding, if you mess up it's hard to recover. I feel like a lot of people are just going to fail it the first time and then when you do, the reset takes so long. There's a cutscene, then a call from Chadley, and when you get back into it then you have to start plodding back to it. 

It feels like encounter design as a dungeon master. You want to mix it up for your players so they're not consistently doing the same thing but you also don't want to do something just to do it. You want to make sure it's individually fun and also makes use of the mechanics and the game they're used to playing and what they've done to build their characters. It should still feel like one cohesive game instead of a complete tangent to the mechanics, style/feel, and arc of the game.
