## The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time
![[2026-11-12 09.52.39.webp]]

We've successfully beaten the dodongos and gotten another sacred gem or whatever. There was a brief moment of confusion here as I couldn't remember the exact sequence. I remembered the shortcut back to the Kokiri Forest through the Lost Woods entrance in Goron City but I couldn't pick up the bombs as a weak child. Then I remembered I could just set them on fire. 

From there, it was pretty straight forward. The Saria scenes always have this bittersweetness to them. Part of it is that she obviously cares so much for Link and he's just kind of a mute doofus who doesn't really respond or seem to appreciate it. I spent some extra time in the Lost Woods after getting Saria's Song to get some of the extra unlockables, the heart piece, expanded ammo capacity, etc. 

![[2026-11-12 10.02.18.webp]]

*Still got them moves.*

The actual Dodongo Cavern was again so simple. I'm not sure when I played this last but the first two dungeons make me reconsider like...were they actually this short? I frequently find a moment here or there where I can't remember exactly how it works, but after a moment it just mentally clicks back into place. I couldn't remember the rhythm for the Dodongo boss, exactly how to get it to breathe fire, was it on a timer or proximity? But once I remembered it stood no chance.

![[2026-11-12 10.44.06.webp]]

*Was really awkward to get these Great Fairy scenes in earshot of your parents...*

Rolled on through Jabu Jabu. This is definitely the trickiest of the three starter dungeons. There's a lot of space to kind of loop through and not know exactly what's happening or where to go next. I remember this one being a little confusing when I was younger. Not so for now me. Barinade is also the toughest of the three starter fights but still not bad at all. The damage I took was mostly not knowing exactly when I could get up close and stab it. 

![[2026-11-12 13.17.12.webp]]

*Jabu Jabu's haunting, dead shark eyes still the same.*

It is weird that you get the boomerang and then it's a kid only item so you don't get to use it that much. I wonder if there was any consideration to making it the second kid dungeon, but then you'd get two ranged weapons right in a row. You also then get the hookshot right after, which is kind of the better boomerang anyways. Curious design decisions. The boomerang is always shafted though. Was by far one of the most optional items in my Link Between Worlds playthrough. 

![[2026-11-12 13.57.19 (2).webp]]

*This scene is burned into my memory.*

It's amazing how, after all this time, so much of this game is just etched into my mind. This game was so epic in scope and formative to me growing up. Things like the music have stuck with me for so long. Not just the hits, Saria's Song, Zelda's Lullaby, Song of Storms, but even just the chanting when you wake up inside the Temple of Light just sent waves of nostalgia over me. Certain scenes like Zelda's flight from the castle are so memorable. The direction on it is really good, the way the camera is positioned does a lot of work here, it's really cinematic.

![[2026-11-12 14.02.52.webp]]

*Iconic.*

After yanking the sword out I beelined it to Kakariko Village to get the hookshot. This was another instance where I knew the broad strokes but not the exact steps. I remembered I needed to chase Dampe in the graveyard, but I wasn't sure how or where exactly. I had gotten the Sun's Song and thought maybe there was an extra room in there I could access now as an adult. Incorrect! I had to walk past those annoying Redead for no reason. 

![[Screenshot_20261112-220800.webp]]

*This was the first time I had to use a bottle fairy. Jerk.*

Checking Dampe's cabin, I found that it was some other grave and got to pushing some headstones around. When I found him, the chase was pretty trivial. Again, another thing I remember being at least moderately difficult, having to try again when you didn't get through one of the gates in time. I sailed right through. 

The Forest Temple is something I heard was streamlined in remake and having done the og now, I'm not disappointed. The number of times I had to abruptly turn around and find another small key was just annoying. They weren't even hard to find, I just didn't expect that I'd need another one so I got to the end of these long hallways and had to backtrack a bunch. 

![[Pasted image 20261112221807.webp]]

*I forgot about this little cutie.*

Most of the poe fights are a little overlong and boring, although I will say that when you have the bow, Hylian Shield, and Master Sword, and you see it all while you're z-targeting you definitely feel like the Hero of Time. The Phantom Ganon fight is always fun, although I always get the better of me. I always assume "that's gotta be the last bounce" and then I just get owned when he bounces it back to me one more than I expect. 

I have a friend who's willing to loan me the remake now that they've beaten it, so I'd really like to push through this and start on that to get a real full comparison. 
## Final Fantasy VII Rebirth
![[2026-11-12 15.36.11.webp]]

*You're mine now!*

Had a bit of time to jump into this, I have, as far as I can tell, one more little world intel thing to do in the first zone. Also did a couple combat simulations and unlocked Titan. It's really not so bad I guess. All the world intel stuff feels so daunting when you have it all in front of you but if you just do it in little chunks here or there it's fine. 

I mean, I say that now in the first zone. 

Also the animations and stuff around it still take too long. But I'm looking forward to checking that last thing off and getting the Midgardsormr. Final Fantasy really is a hodge podge of fantastical elements. 

I feel like I might play FFVII original after this too. I mean, might as well hit up all these monumental classics of the late 90s.
