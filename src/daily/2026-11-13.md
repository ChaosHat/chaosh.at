## The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time
![[2026-11-13 11.28.09.webp]]

We got to and beat the Fire Temple. I spent some amount of time again, slightly uncertain about which way to go. I knew I needed to go to Death Mountain, so I immediately hauled ass for the top, waved hi to Biggoron, and went in and...well there was nothing. Hm. Eventually I remembered I had to go to Goron City and go blast Goron Link to proceed. 

The dungeon itself is fine. Again, this is something where streamlining wouldn't bum me out at all, and maybe this is just the fact that I've already done it a bunch and I mostly know how to do this so the joy of discovery is lost on me. It's mostly pretty straightforward as is, but I can see how a player could just get totally lost and why that's a bit of a downside. 

I wonder if that's a bigger problem today. When I was a kid this was the new game I had and if I got frustrated, well I didn't have another new option to go to, or endless novelty in my pocket via a smartphone/the internet. I wonder if designers work with the thought "if someone gets stuck more than 10 minutes they're just going to quit."  

![[2026-11-13 12.06.45.webp]]

*Yaaaaaawn.*

It's interesting to me how much of Zelda's difficulty design is based on figuring out the puzzle. Essentially every boss is trivial once you know its schtick and Volvagia is no different. I spent a lot of the fight pretty bored. Oh yes, you're flying, so scaaaaary. Now come back here so I can hit you with this hammer again and put us both out of our misery.

The water temple definitely sucks. Its problem is that it's not that hard, it's just extremely tedious and the punishment for being wrong is so much time. On top of that, the idiot amoeba boss is also annoying. It just moves a lot and it's hard to hit with your stupid longshot that keeps you frozen in one place for a long time, and then on top of that when it hits you the animation also takes forever. It's like they got all the bad ideas out in one dungeon.
## Final Fantasy VII Rebirth
![[2026-11-13 14.07.13.webp]]

*I just realized this is the second giant snake monster I fought today.*

I got through chapter two no problem after quickly wrapping up the last world intel fight I needed to do. With that, and grabbing Barret's newest weapon, it was time to fight the Midgardsormr. The fight was overall easy, I think I might up the difficulty to dynamic or something, maybe I'm a bit overleveled for the content? It just seems like the fight ends before I get to do my limit breaks and summons and all the cool stuff.

With it defeated, we got to see our mysterious Sephiroth appearance. Is he here? Is he not here? If he's not here, who impaled the snake then?! This is the part that I'm most interested in seeing resolved by actually beating this this time, but I have a feeling they probably punt on it for part three. What *is* Sephiroth's whole deal?

![[2026-11-13 14.14.06.webp]]

*Hmmm, mysterious.*

Chapter three is pretty straightforward and linear, it's a nice break from the open world stuff. We get another fight with the Turks who are terrible people but damn I just can't help but be amused by them. I don't know exactly what it is. Good job by whoever wrote these dorks. Although maybe Rude hard carries this group. Reno is kind of a douche. 

![[2026-11-13 14.51.44.webp]]

*This cute little tryhard.*

When I got to the segment where you only control Barret and Red XIII I went oh yeah, this section. I'm not sure why they think Barret can just shoot his way out of every problem. Rocks in the way? Time for gun baby. That just seems dangerous. And I suppose credit to the team for just totally handwaving bullets.

A good part about the split party section here is that it does a lot to show the camaraderie of the team. The party bantering about who owes what is fun, shows how they've grown together, and how far they've come. It wasn't that long ago that cloud would sincerely be charging for it, and that Barret would just be pissed off about it.
