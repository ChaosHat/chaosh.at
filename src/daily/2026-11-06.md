I'm intentionally burying the lede here a bit for spoilers, but I beat Final Fantasy Resonance, see that below! I don't know what to play next, my backlog is so long. I want to play Monster Hunter Stories 3 since it's still a this year release, and I have Prince of Persia: The Lost Crown to finish, and Well Dweller as another new Metroidvania. However...
## Final Fantasy VII Rebirth
![[2909400_20261106220001_1.webp]]

*Yep, we're going back.*

I think this is my window to play FFVII Rebirth. I want to give myself enough time to play through it and also not be totally burnt out on the game and its mechanics before it comes out next year. I think I can alternate it and one of the Metroidvanias. 

I've actually played maybe two thirds, or maybe three quarters of this on the PS5 but got fatigued. I think I did too much of the side questing and it led to some burn out. So I'm not going to do that this time! 

![[2909400_20261106221004_1.webp]]

*Hey, didn't I earn you as visions?*

So, we begin at the beginning! It's been nearly three months since I re-finished Remake and I feel pretty ready to go. Immediately, this game feels just like being welcomed back by old friends. The music and the characters are just so immediately familiar.

The intro of this game does a good job of setting up the confusion and immediate interest in what's different in this story. Getting going with the Zack side just a bit and then immediately going back to our primary reality(?) is a strong tease. I know from the scuttlebutt that the ending of this middle chapter of the remake trilogy is maybe not the most well received, but I'm optimistic and hopeful that's mostly just people who are disappointed it's not a shot for shot remake in 4K HDR.

![[2909400_20261106222902_1.webp]]

*A photo to commemorate our journey.*

The initial Nibelheim section is so good at triggering my memories of the original too. I can just see the original cutscene of Cloud checking in with his mom, the music is the idealized version of what I remember, it's how it sounded to me back then. This section is also always so bittersweet in hindsight, knowing what you know about Cloud. Rebirth does a better job of selling Tifa's unwillingness to correct Cloud's recollection of events too. 
## Final Fantasy Resonance
![[3259780_20261106115943_1.webp]]

I beat this! I wrapped up a few things before going to the crystal tower. I got a couple more weapons and a piece of armor at the special weapon boss fight thing before finishing the second tier of the colosseum. After that we got in the airship and pushed the critical path to the end. I'm going to talk about some spoilers below.

Plot wise, I really liked how Raegan didn't die at the end and we mostly got a happy ending. I thought we would get a "sorry Rain, I have to die to stop this you made me proud son" scene. I felt bad for Archwitch Fina, but she did get a noble ending. I liked her and her design a lot and now she's gone. C'est la vie. 

I maintain it's unsatisfying for Sol to swoop in and be the bad guy out of nowhere right at the end. There's just no connection to him, he's very generic, and unlike the Sworn Six his motivation is totally unsympathetic. I mean, the Sworn Six's goal is also insane, but you can see how 700 years of anger would cause you to go insane.

![[3259780_20261106141523_1.webp]]

*RAR IT'S A SCARY MONSTER*

As far as the other characters, I liked Jake and Lid's whole vibe and how they grew closer. It felt very Han and Leia although I just googled their age gap and, ugh. Why? Just make it age appropriate. Nichol was flatter as a character but awesome as a unit. Sakura never really landed for me, I'm not a fan of the character that looks like a child but is secretly ancient but also loves candy archetype. Rain is your typical JRPG protagonist who is unflappably upbeat and sunny and will defeat all foes with truth, justice, and hope, and the performance makes him earnest, not annoying. I liked Lasswell's kinder, gentler Vegeta vibe, but like Nichol he doesn't really jump off the page.

The plot and characters are ultimately not doing anything super out of the ordinary. It has those standard Final Fantasy beats, its fair share of twists, and then of course, for some reason, at the end you have to fight god or the physical manifestation of an abstract concept. Of course it has to do that, it's a love letter to Final Fantasy. 

![[3259780_20261106150920_1.webp]]

*This jerk.*

Mechanically, this game has the exact right amount of depth for what it is. You can interact with the systems really deeply and do broken stuff, or you can not! There are a lot of options to play with and you really get to author and build your characters. My complaints would be mostly a combination of width/balance. There are so many options it can be overwhelming but it isn't actually once you look into it because so many visions just aren't good at all. Some decisions are just correct: you have to get the MRP increase, everyone needs double actions, etc. If more visions were more viable, I think that's when it would start to feel overwhelming.

![[3259780_20261106152403_1.webp]]

*It's raining Rain.*

And again, it's not so hard that you *need to* take the correct path but it is so glaringly optimal and honestly waaaaay more fun. Combat was a lot more interesting and engaging when I had multiple actions. A lot of things also felt balanced around you doing certain things and at least exploiting it some. Mana costs were tough until I could halve them. I think people just playing and building characters based on vibes would definitely have a more difficult time. 

Overall though, I think this game is a lot like what I wanted Dragon Quest XI S to be. It's a very comfy cozy, straight down the middle JRPG but this has just a little more going for it mechanically and a really unique and lovely art style. This game feels like a B+++++++. It might be the best a game can be at just executing on something known and good.
